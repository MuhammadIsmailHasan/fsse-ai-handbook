## 🎯 What you'll learn

- Choosing **target roles** and a **target company list** instead of applying everywhere
- Where junior tech roles appear: Indonesian job portals, LinkedIn, company career pages, communities, remote boards
- **Referrals** and outreach messages that get answers
- A weekly **application funnel** with numbers you can improve
- Tracking every application (use your own CareerPilot)
- Recognising **job scams** and bad offers

## 🧠 The big idea

A job search is a **sales pipeline**, and you are both the product and the salesperson. Sales teams don't wait for customers; they pick target accounts, reach out, track every conversation and fix the stage where deals get stuck. Do the same: *applications → responses → interviews → offers*, measured weekly.

## ❓ Why it matters

"Apply to 200 jobs with one click" feels productive and usually produces silence. A focused search — a target list, tailored applications, referrals and follow-ups — gets more interviews for less effort, and tells you *why* things aren't working.

Treating it as a funnel is what makes it fixable. If 50 applications give 2 replies, the problem is the CV or the targeting. If 10 interviews give no offers, the problem is in the interview, not the CV. Guessing costs months.

If you're aiming for **remote roles outside Indonesia, especially in the US**, the same funnel applies with different inputs: remote-first job boards, companies that already hire in Asia, an English CV, and a clear line about your time-zone overlap. Fewer, better-matched applications beat volume even more strongly there.

## 📖 Step by step

### 1. Targets

| Decide | Example |
|---|---|
| Role titles (2–3) | Junior Backend Engineer (Python) · Junior Full-Stack Developer · Software Engineer (Graduate/Entry) |
| Company types | product startups, scale-ups, tech consultancies/software houses, banks & fintech tech teams, multinational tech hubs, remote-first companies |
| Location/work mode | Jakarta hybrid, Bandung, remote (Indonesia), remote (APAC) |
| Must-haves | mentorship, code review, stack you want, minimum salary you've researched |

Build a list of **30–50 target companies**. For each: career page, tech stack (job posts, engineering blogs), 1–2 engineers to follow.

### 2. Where to look

For remote-US roles, prioritise companies that already employ people outside the US — they have the paperwork and the working habits, so your application isn't the one that creates a new problem for them.

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| **LinkedIn Jobs** | set alerts by title + location; watch "posted in the last week" |
| **Indonesian portals**: JobStreet, Glints, Kalibrr, Dealls, Karir.com | many startup and junior roles; set alerts |
| **Company career pages** | often posted there first; apply directly |
| **Communities**: tech meetups, Discord/Telegram groups, bootcamp alumni networks | job leads and referrals |
| **Remote boards**: Wellfound, Remote OK, We Work Remotely, Himalayas | competitive; stronger portfolio and English needed |
| **Software houses / outsourcing firms** | often hire juniors; ask about training and project rotation |

Check recent postings of each company to see what they actually use.

### 3. Referrals and outreach

A referral usually gets your CV **read** by a human — it doesn't guarantee an interview. Earn it:

```text
Hi Andi — I saw Kopi Data is hiring a Junior Backend Engineer (Python). I've been building CareerPilot, a Flask + PostgreSQL job-tracker API with 113 tests (link). I read your post on moving to FastAPI and would love 10 minutes to ask what the team values in juniors. If it seems like a fit, would you be open to referring me?
```

- Personalise (their post, their team), show proof (link), make a small ask, and follow up **once** after ~5 working days.
- Alumni from your bootcamp or university are the warmest contacts.

### 4. The weekly funnel

Write the numbers down each week. Without them, a bad month feels like bad luck; with them, it points at the exact stage to fix.

| Stage | Weekly target (example) | If it's low… |
|---|---|---|
| Tailored applications | 10–15 | spend less time on Easy Apply, more on target list |
| Referral/outreach messages | 5 | improve personalisation |
| Responses | ≥ 15% of applications | CV/LinkedIn/portfolio or role mismatch — get reviews |
| First interviews | 2–3 | practice screening calls and pitch |
| Technical rounds passed | ≥ 50% | DSA/live coding/take-home practice |
| Offers | — | negotiation lesson |

Review every Friday: which stage is weakest? Fix that stage next week.

### 5. Track it — in your own app

Use **CareerPilot**: companies, applications, statuses (saved → applied → screening → interview → offer), notes after each call, and the dashboard as your funnel. It's also a great interview story: "I built the tool I used for my job search."

### 6. Scams and red flags

Real employers don't ask you to pay for training, equipment or "processing". Anything that requires money from you, or rushes you through a chat app, is a scam.

- Paying for "training" or "equipment" before starting; "bond" contracts with large penalties for leaving.
- Interviews only over chat, offers without interviews, requests for KTP/bank details early.
- Recruiters using personal emails for "big companies"; job posts copied from real companies with different contact info.
- Unpaid "trial projects" that look like real client work.

Verify the company (website, LinkedIn employees, official career page) before sharing documents.

### Choosing your setup

Every option here is a trade-off:

| | ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|---|
| Mass applying | volume | low response, no learning |
| Targeted applications | higher response, better fit | fewer per week |
| Remote-only | bigger salary range, flexibility | much more competition, time zones |
| Software houses | junior-friendly, broad exposure | varying mentorship; project pressure |
| Startups | ownership, fast learning | less structure, more risk |

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- Waiting to feel "ready" before applying — apply when you meet ~60–70% of requirements.
- Ignoring non-startup employers (banks, telcos, logistics, government-tech vendors) that hire many developers.
- One CV for everything.
- Not preparing after getting a response.

## 🏆 Best practices

- [ ] 2–3 target titles and a 30–50 company list.
- [ ] Alerts on LinkedIn and 2–3 Indonesian portals; check career pages weekly.
- [ ] Tailor CV per application; apply within a few days of posting.
- [ ] 5 personalised outreach messages per week; follow up once.
- [ ] Track everything (CareerPilot) and review the funnel weekly.
- [ ] Protect your time and data: verify companies, never pay to get a job.
- [ ] Keep learning and building during the search (1–2 hours a day) — it gives you new stories.
