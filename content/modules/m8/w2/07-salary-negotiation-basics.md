::part[Learn the Concept]{n="1"}

## 🎯 What you'll learn

- Researching realistic **salary ranges** for your role, city and company type
- Answering "What are your salary expectations?" at each stage
- Reading an **Indonesian offer**: gross vs take-home, THR, BPJS, probation, contract type, allowances
- Comparing offers by **total value**, not only base salary
- A polite, evidence-based **negotiation script** (email and call)
- Negotiating **non-salary** items and deciding when to accept

## 🧠 The big idea

Negotiation is **collaborative problem solving**, not a fight. The company wants to hire you (they've invested hours in interviews); you want to join. You're both looking for a package that works. Coming with research, clear priorities and a friendly tone makes the conversation easy for the recruiter to champion internally.

## ❓ Why does this matter?

Your first salary anchors future raises and offers. Small differences compound over years. Many juniors accept the first number out of fear — but a respectful, well-reasoned request rarely causes an offer to be withdrawn at reputable companies.

## 💥 What happens if we DON'T?

- Saying a number in the first call without research → far below the budget for the role.
- Comparing a PKWT 1-year contract with a permanent offer only by monthly salary.
- Accepting verbally, then discovering the offer is net of different deductions than you assumed.

## 📖 How it works, step by step

### 1. Research ranges

- Sources: salary insights on job portals (Glints, Kalibrr, JobStreet, LinkedIn), Glassdoor, tech salary reports published by recruitment agencies, community surveys, alumni and friends in similar roles.
- Segment: **role** (backend/frontend/full-stack), **level** (junior), **city** (Jakarta vs other cities), **company type** (startup, multinational, bank, software house), **remote** (local vs foreign company).
- Write down **three numbers**: *walk-away* (minimum you'd accept), *target* (realistic good), *stretch* (top of range). Check data is recent — ranges change yearly.

### 2. The expectations question

| Stage | Approach |
|---|---|
| Application form requires a number | enter your researched **target** (or a range whose bottom is your target) |
| Recruiter screen | "Based on my research for junior full-stack roles in Jakarta, I'm looking at **X–Y** gross per month, depending on the full package. What's the budgeted range for this role?" |
| Pushed for current salary (career switcher) | "My previous role was in a different field, so it's not a good comparison — I'm focused on market rates for this position." |

Always clarify **gross or net (take-home)** and **monthly or annual**.

### 3. Reading an Indonesian offer

| Item | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Base salary** | gross monthly; income tax (PPh 21) and BPJS employee contributions are deducted → take-home is lower; ask for an estimated take-home |
| **THR** (Tunjangan Hari Raya) | religious holiday allowance, generally one month's salary after 12 months of service (pro-rated for shorter service) — required by regulation for eligible employees |
| **BPJS Kesehatan & Ketenagakerjaan** | social security for health and employment (old-age, work accident, death, pension); company and employee contributions |
| **Private health insurance** | outpatient/inpatient, family coverage, dental/optical |
| **Contract type** | **PKWTT** (permanent, may include probation up to 3 months) vs **PKWT** (fixed-term contract; duration, renewal, conversion to permanent) |
| **Probation** | length, salary during probation, evaluation criteria |
| **Allowances & bonus** | transport, meal, internet/remote allowance, annual bonus (guaranteed or discretionary?), performance review cycle |
| **Equity / ESOP** (startups) | amount, vesting schedule, cliff, strike price — treat as uncertain |
| **Work mode & hours** | hybrid days, overtime policy, on-call |
| **Growth** | learning budget, mentorship, promotion timeline |
| **Notice period / penalties** | resignation notice; any training bonds or penalties (read carefully) |

Laws and rules change; confirm current regulations and ask HR for written clarification when unsure.

### 4. Compare total value

```text
Offer A (startup, PKWTT): Rp X gross/month, 3-month probation, private insurance, remote allowance, annual bonus up to 1 month, learning budget
Offer B (software house, PKWT 12 months): Rp X+10% gross/month, BPJS only, no bonus, renewal uncertain
```

Annualize: 12 × base + THR + expected bonus + allowances + insurance value. Then weigh **learning**, mentorship, stability and commute.

### 5. Negotiation script

Email after receiving the written offer:

```text
Hi Sari,

Thank you for the offer for the Junior Full-Stack Engineer role — I'm excited about the team and the product.

Before I sign, I'd like to discuss the base salary. Based on my research for junior full-stack roles in Jakarta and the scope we discussed (API and Next.js features, on-call rotation after probation), I was expecting around Rp Y gross per month. Would there be flexibility to move the base closer to that?

If the base is fixed, I'd also be happy to discuss a remote-work allowance or a salary review after probation.

Thanks again — looking forward to your thoughts.
Best regards,
Ismail
```

Principles: be appreciative, give a **specific number with reasons**, ask one clear question, offer alternatives, then **stop talking** and wait. Negotiate once, not in endless rounds.

### 6. Beyond salary

Start date, remote days, learning budget, laptop choice, **salary review after probation** (in writing), title, extra leave days, relocation help.

### 7. Deciding

- Get everything in **writing** before resigning elsewhere or declining other processes.
- Use deadlines honestly: "I have another process finishing next week — could I respond by Friday?"
- Decline politely; keep relationships — the recruiter may contact you in a year.

## ⚖️ Trade-offs

| | ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|---|
| Saying a number first | shows preparation, sets anchor | risk of anchoring too low without research |
| Asking for their range first | information advantage | some recruiters insist |
| Negotiating | often better package | small risk at companies with fixed bands (usually just "no") |
| Accepting immediately | quick certainty | may leave value on the table |

## 🏆 Best practices

- [ ] Research ranges by role, level, city and company type; know walk-away/target/stretch.
- [ ] Always clarify gross vs net and monthly vs annual.
- [ ] Negotiate after the written offer, politely, with a specific number and reasons.
- [ ] Compare total compensation and learning opportunities.
- [ ] Read the contract type, probation, bonds and notice period carefully.
- [ ] Get agreements in writing; never lie about competing offers.
- [ ] Stay professional whether you accept or decline.

## 🏭 How it's used in industry

- Many companies have salary **bands** per level; recruiters often have some room within a band and can escalate with good reasons.
- Structured offers (base + allowances + bonus + equity) are common in Indonesian startups; banks and multinationals often have fixed grades with standard benefits.
- Remote roles for foreign companies may pay in other currencies or through employer-of-record services — check taxes and contracts.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes

- Negotiating before you have an offer.
- Using ultimatums or fake competing offers.
- Ignoring probation terms and contract type.
- Forgetting take-home calculations and assuming gross = net.

## 🗣️ Explain it

Explain why negotiation is "collaborative problem solving" and why a polite, researched request rarely hurts.

## 📐 Design it

Prepare your negotiation sheet: researched range with 3 sources, walk-away/target/stretch numbers, your top 3 non-salary priorities, your recruiter-screen answer, and a negotiation email for a hypothetical offer 10% below your target.

:::quiz
Show a checklist for your sheet

- **Range sources:** at least 3, dated within the last year, matching role/level/city.
- **Numbers:** walk-away based on your real monthly costs + savings goals; target from the middle of the researched range; stretch from the upper part.
- **Priorities:** e.g. mentorship & code review, hybrid ≤ 3 office days, learning budget.
- **Screen answer:** range + "depending on the full package" + ask for their budget.
- **Email:** thanks → enthusiasm → specific number with reasons → one clear ask → alternative (review after probation) → friendly close.
:::
